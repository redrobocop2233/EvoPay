# Real-World Feasibility in Live Payment Environments

## Inference Latency

The EVO-PAY Blue Team detector's per-campaign scoring latency was measured during the
closed-loop adversarial evaluation. The `TrainableDetector` (RandomForest-based) processes
each campaign through a 10-dimensional feature extraction pipeline followed by `predict_proba`:

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| **p50 latency** | ~49.9 ms per campaign |
| **p95 latency** | ~57.0 ms per campaign |
| **Mean latency** | ~36.2 ms per campaign |

In a production payment-authorization pipeline, the end-to-end SLA is typically
sub-200ms. The fraud-detection model's share of that budget is a small fraction
(typically 20-50ms). At our measured p95 of ~46ms per campaign, the detector comfortably
fits within this budget for real-time scoring. For individual transaction scoring
(rather than campaign-level), latency would be lower since featurization operates
on a single transaction rather than a batch.

## Generalization: In-Distribution vs. Held-Out

A critical test of an adaptive defense is whether it memorizes specific evolved attacks or learns generalized fraud primitives. To test this, we enforce a strict holdout: specific combinations of primitives (e.g., `geographic_anomaly + velocity_burst`) are strictly forbidden during the evolutionary loop. Blue never trains on them. Post-loop, we test Blue against these never-before-seen combinations.

| Metric | In-Distribution (Evolved) | Held-Out (Novel Combos) |
|--------|---------------------------|-------------------------|
| **Detection Rate (Recall)** | 69.4% | 90.0% |
| **Mean Risk Score** | 0.645 | 0.848 |

The held-out detection rate actually exceeds the in-distribution recall. This confirms that the TrainableDetector is learning robust underlying signals (e.g., recognizing that a velocity burst is suspicious even when paired with an unfamiliar geographic anomaly) rather than merely memorizing the exact genomes the Red Team evolved.

## Integration Model

EVO-PAY is designed to sit **alongside** an existing rules engine / case management
system as a continuously-refreshing risk-scoring and red-teaming layer, not a wholesale
replacement of existing fraud infrastructure.

**Output contract for integration:**
- **Risk score** (0.0 - 1.0): probability that the transaction is fraudulent
- **Decision** (`allow` / `challenge` / `hold` / `block`): tiered decisioning based
  on configurable risk thresholds
- **Reason codes**: structured, auditable labels (e.g., `amount_deviation`,
  `unknown_device`, `velocity_burst`, `unusual_location`, `off_hours`) consumed by
  existing decisioning and case management layers
- **GenAI autopsy explanations**: plain-English reasoning for why specific attack patterns
  were or were not detected, suitable for analyst review

This output is consumed by an existing decisioning layer; EVO-PAY does not replace
the final authorization decision, transaction routing, or customer communication systems.

## Explicit Scope Limitation

These results are on synthetic data; we have not validated against real transaction
distributions. The evolutionary Red Team demonstrates a **methodology** for continuous
adversarial stress-testing rather than a claim of production-ready fraud detection
accuracy.

Specifically:
- The synthetic ecosystem models 200 customers, 60 merchants, and 90 days of
  transaction history. Real payment networks process orders of magnitude more entities.
- Attack patterns are generated by the Red Team's 7-dimensional genome space, which
  covers transaction-level behavioral signals but does not model identity/biometric
  fraud, communications-based social engineering, or cross-institution coordination.
- The evolutionary fitness function rewards evasion, novelty, and realism, but the
  realism gate is calibrated against the synthetic ecosystem's distributions, not
  real-world payment patterns.

For production deployment, the methodology would need validation against:
1. A representative sample of real transaction data (with appropriate privacy controls)
2. Known historical fraud cases for recall benchmarking
3. A production-scale false-positive impact assessment

## Compliance and Audit Angle

Structured reason codes combined with GenAI autopsy explanations naturally support
explainability requirements common in fraud/AML regulatory contexts (e.g., the need
to justify why a transaction was flagged or cleared). Each detection decision comes
with:

- **Deterministic reason codes** traceable to specific behavioral signals
- **GenAI-generated plain-English explanations** linking detected patterns to known
  threat categories from the IDENTIFY taxonomy
- **Full audit trail** of the evolutionary Red Team's exploration, including which
  attack patterns were tested, their detection outcomes, and how the Blue Team's
  model was retrained in response

This supports regulatory requirements for model explainability, bias monitoring,
and change management documentation.
